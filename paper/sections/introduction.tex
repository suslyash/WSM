\section{Introduction}

Behavioral depression and Parkinson recognition is attractive because speech, movement, and visual behavior can contain complementary signals. It is also easy to overstate: the available depression and Parkinson corpora carry different observed labels, so a missing task label is unknown rather than negative. Treating the combined data as a three-class healthy/depression/Parkinson problem would rule out simultaneous predictions and manufacture negative labels for unobserved diseases.

We present WSM as a methods-and-evidence study of this partial-label setting. The target is two independent binary variables, depression and Parkinson, with an observed-label mask. We freeze the full research record before final Test reporting: model roles, five seeds, checkpoints, selection metric, and paired summary plan are all fixed from DEV evidence. The final Test is a one-shot generalization report rather than another model-selection set.

The paper makes three bounded contributions. First, it specifies a masked multitask audio--video formulation with reliability-weighted direct pseudo-supervision for missing task entries. Second, it reports a complete five-seed comparison among frozen temporal audio, full R4, and an equal-parameter shared-fusion candidate. Third, it records both positive and negative ablation outcomes instead of presenting every engineered component as necessary. The primary candidate is shared fusion because the DEV-only parsimony decision found no paired Mean confidence-interval separation from full R4 while retaining equal parameter count. This designation is not a statistical-superiority claim and was not revised after Test reporting.
