\begin{abstract}
Depression and Parkinson classification from in-the-wild behavioral media is complicated by disjoint corpora: a depression example is not annotated for Parkinson, and a Parkinson example is not annotated for depression. We frame this setting as two independent binary tasks with masked unknown labels rather than a mutually exclusive disease classifier. The completed WSM evidence program evaluates a frozen temporal-audio baseline, a full task-conditioned audio--video model (R4), and an equal-parameter shared-fusion model over five seeds. The primary paper candidate is the shared-fusion model, selected before final Test reporting by a DEV-only parsimony decision; it is not selected by a claim of statistical superiority over full R4. On the frozen five-seed DEV summary, shared fusion obtains a Mean\_Score of 0.790914 and full R4 obtains 0.790057, compared with 0.765145 for temporal audio. A single frozen final evaluation reports shared-fusion Mean\_Scores of 0.806003, 0.815349, and 0.826483 on TEST\_NONE, TEST\_SOFT, and TEST\_HARD, respectively. Every predeclared paired final-Test Mean confidence interval includes zero. Controlled ablations support direct pseudo-supervision, uncertainty/reliability handling, the online audio branch, and sample-specific pseudo alignment under its narrow negative control; several other proposed mechanisms are not supported. We report the evidence conservatively: it does not verify missing-label correctness, comorbidity recovery, or clinical validity of pseudo labels.
\end{abstract}
